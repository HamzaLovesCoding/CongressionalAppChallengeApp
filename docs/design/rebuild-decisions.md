# Lane rebuild: rationale and corrections

These are design rationales and audience hypotheses, not private reasoning traces or findings from user testing.

| Direction | Basis | Likely appeal | Likely objections |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original rounded earthy app | Existing screen: green, cream, mascots, rewards, multiple Home sections | Teens who enjoy collectible characters and approachable game-like learning | Too many competing actions; decorative characters can feel childish; faint boundaries weaken hierarchy |
| Supplied league reference | User-provided image; clear rows, substantial navigation, strong grouping | Users familiar with social and competitive mobile apps | Ranking emphasis can distract from learning; its colors were not requested for Lane |
| Strong bordered interface | Inferred from the request for rigid, confident boundaries | Users who want clear controls and an obvious hierarchy | Outlining every nested section would resemble a developer dashboard and add clutter |
| Flat minimal implementation | My overcorrection toward single-plane surfaces and less content | Users seeking a quiet utility with little decoration | Lost Lane's visual identity and felt like a generic website; this matched the user's criticism |
| Current rebuild | User selected both earthy/richer road imagery and rounded controls/gamification, plus permission to redesign navigation while retaining all five destinations | Teens who want a recognizable mobile app, driving-specific visuals, clear actions, and optional avatar rewards | Cream/ochre and avatars will not suit everyone; decorative imagery still needs restraint; its appeal needs user testing |

## Where the previous pass missed the request

- Reducing clutter became excessive visual reduction. A plain wordmark and basic rows weakened the app's identity instead of making the original design clearer.
- The progress car was initially a simple marker beside a straight list. It did not fully deliver the requested sense of driving along a pathway.
- Moving the leaderboard to Profile was my interpretation of the requested Home simplification, not an explicit instruction to put it there.
- Broad styling claims exceeded the structural changes: the simulator and summary had mainly received styling, and the copy audit was incomplete.
- Saying the whole visual request was handled was too broad while those gaps remained.

## What the rebuild changes

The comparison workbench keeps the original and the rejected minimal Home beside a new composition. It began with a blank third canvas and now embeds the live rebuilt app. The original wordmark and driving visual return; Home retains the requested one main action, compact progress and lesson/saved links. The map becomes a winding highway with a moving car. The five navigation destinations remain, with Feed in the center. Profile keeps the avatars, while its stats and collection get a deliberate mobile layout. Practice, quiz, summary and simulator controls receive replacement component styles and revised presentation. Existing behavior, rule content and persistence remain connected.

This is implemented design work, not evidence of competition readiness. The simulator still has one scenario, the feed still lacks licensed lesson videos, and real multi-user rankings still need accounts/backend work.
